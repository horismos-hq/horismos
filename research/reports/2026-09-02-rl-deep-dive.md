## TL;DR

**Verdict on the RL axis: aligned-with-adjustments** (same vocabulary as sweep #2, scoped to this axis; §4.1 has the full table).

1. **The field's motion confirms the pivot's shape.** The 2026 RL literature's most wanted object is a *deterministic symbolic step-checker* usable as a process reward: three independent 2026 papers (Cochrane decision-tree verifiers, Lean tactic verdicts, symbolic game oracles) beat both outcome-only verifiable rewards and learned process-reward models with exactly that, and a fourth shows derivation-based credit assignment through a typed layer beating RL by 27.8 points. Horismos's five-check gate, proof trees, and deterministic outcome labels are that object, produced as a side effect of normal operation, for any loaded ontology. Nobody in the surveyed literature uses an RDFS/OWL/SHACL/Datalog kernel as a reward oracle for an LLM policy; the square is open.
2. **The "traces are output = training corpus" claim is half true today.** The representation half (versioned schema, per-step `loss_mask`, provenance, determinism) is built and disciplined. The signal half is not: `outcome.labels` carries one key, gate-refused perception attempts are not traced at all, the gate's domain-term witness is never fed back on retry (contrary to doc 06), no exporter exists, and no replay exists. These are small, additive fixes, and they are the prerequisite for every RL use. Two further corrections: the field's trainers consume token-level masks (the model-output/scaffold boundary falls inside a step once a chat template is applied), and doc 09's premise that the TIR/CIR literature is "unanimous" on masking executor output is wrong — the corpus whose tag family `EXPORT_TAGS` mirrors (OpenMathInstruct-1) explicitly does not mask; the choice still matches the RL-side majority.
3. **Outcome labels alone will not train a better perception model.** A measured result (Perception-R1: accuracy-only RLVR fails to improve perception, McNemar p = 0.22–0.69) plus the verifier-quality literature say the per-fact gate verdict is the signal that matters, and that the verifier must be strong (a weakened deterministic oracle actively hurts; schema-only validation has a 0.87 false-positive rate). PRP-005's BDD verifier and non-vacuity pass are therefore preconditions for using the gate as a reward, not adjacent features.
4. **RL-driven ontology learning does not weaken the human gate; it strengthens it.** Across 18 LLM ontology-learning papers, three LLMs4OL editions, and the 2026 "self-refining ontology" flagship (OaK), there is zero RL on the schema layer; where RL touches structure it optimizes an LLM-judged utility, not truth, and RL over formal artifacts is empirically brittle (mode collapse; SFT beating GRPO on text-to-SPARQL). The credible counter-proposal is a *staged* gate (ANNEAL: automated scoring + canary + rollback for low-risk additive patches), not gate removal. A "learnable ontology, gated commit" architecture is sketched in §4.3 and is buildable on the kernel's determinism (forked-World replay gives exact counterfactual scoring for free).
5. **The 2026 integration shape is "be an environment," not "export a JSONL."** OpenEnv (Gymnasium-style `reset/step/state`, typed actions/observations, a technical committee spanning Meta, Hugging Face, Prime Intellect, Nvidia), Prime Intellect's `verifiers`/Environments Hub, Agent Lightning's "the harness owns the loop" proxy capture, and OpenAI RFT's `python` grader all converge on a deterministic reward function behind a small interface. Horismos's most useful RL-axis move is an environment/grader surface, with the doc-09 exporter as a thin adapter over it.
6. **Frontier-lab internal practice is mostly not public.** What is measured: OpenAI RFT's grader taxonomy, OpenAI's and Anthropic's reward-hacking findings from real coding-RL runs, DeepMind's verifier-driven search systems. What is not: environment formats, reward mixes, or any use of third-party symbolic verifiers. The report says so rather than guessing (§3.5).

**Ranked moves** (§4.4): (1) close the trace signal gap [S, PRP-004]; (2) a Gymnasium/OpenEnv-style `horismos` environment + grader surface [M, owner open]; (3) the doc-09 exporter as an adapter over it [S, owner open]; (4) publish the gate's error rates and an isomorphic-relabeling invariance test as PRP-005 acceptance items [M, PRP-005]; (5) the gated ontology-learning sandbox, after PRP-005/008 [L, PRP-007]. Do **not** build: a differentiable/semiring Type-5 extension, RL fine-tuning of the authoring agent before SFT on a verified authoring corpus, an LLM-judge process reward, auto-admission of ontology changes in v1, or a trainer.

**[inference]** Net: the *representation* half of "traces are the corpus" is real and

trainer would consume, the negative examples a verifier-reward needs, and any exporter or
environment surface — is spec-only. That gap, not the design, is what decides whether the
flywheel claim is true today.

## 3. Landscape (live sweep, 2026-09-02)

Each subsection gives the measured state of the field, then what it says to Horismos as tagged inference. Numbers are quoted from the cited source; where a sub-sweep could only see a snippet the row is marked **[unverified]**.

### 3.1 Learnable ontologies via RL

**Headline [inference]**: the "RL drives structure learning" thread is real and accelerating, but it is aimed at instance-level graphs, memory contents, and retrieval policies, not at the schema/TBox. Every surveyed system that mutates an *ontology* uses an LLM judge, a validation loop, or a human — never a reward gradient.

**RL where reward drives graph structure (schema stays fixed)**

- **[measured]** AutoGraph-R1 (HKUST et al., ACL 2026 long; [arXiv:2510.15339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15339)) trains a Qwen2.5-3B/7B "KG constructor" with RL against downstream RAG utility: +2.36 avg F1 with a subgraph retriever, +2.97 with Think-on-Graph, +1.35 with HippoRAG. The reward is a **DeepSeek-V3 LLM judge** ("is the gold answer deducible from the constructed KG") plus passage recall; the paper's own framing is "useful graphs, not good graphs." No verifier, no human gate.
- **[measured]** "Knowledge Graphs are Implicit Reward Models" (Kansal & Jha, Princeton, Jan 2026; [arXiv:2601.15160](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15160v1)): a 14B model trained on 1–3-hop UMLS paths reaches **89.33% on unseen 5-hop** questions (base Qwen3-14B 19.94% on difficulty-5; GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Pro decline with hop count). Reward = binary correctness plus a deterministic **path-alignment** term (entity coverage of the reasoning trace against the gold KG path, ≥2-entity floor, repetition penalty). The ontology is hand-curated and fixed; causation runs ontology → reward.
- **[measured]** Text-to-SPARQL with GRPO on DBLP (Pfeifer, Banerjee, Usbeck, May 2026; [arXiv:2605.20066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20066)): GRPO beats zero-shot, but **supervised DoRA fine-tuning beats GRPO at the same scale**; the execution-based reward carries most of the gain.
- **[unverified]** GRAPH-GRPO-LEX ([arXiv:2511.06618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06618)): contract-graph extraction under a *given* legal ontology with graph-metric rewards.

**The only two systems that RL-train a model to author a formal artifact**

- **[measured]** POntAvignon ([arXiv:2512.07452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07452), Dec 2025): >98% correct RDF-triple extraction for performing-arts ephemera with "formal and semantic rewards" — the abstract does not define "formal" (syntax validity vs ontology conformance is unresolved). Single domain workflow.
- **[measured]** BPMN process models via RL ([arXiv:2607.06175](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06175v1), Jul 2026): GSPO on Llama 3.1 8B / Qwen 2.5 14B with a 38-metric composite reward (16 syntactic metrics from the BPMN spec, 15 pragmatic, 7 semantic) plus an invalidity penalty; pragmatic quality +0.11, variance cut sixfold vs SFT. **Equal reward weighting beat targeted emphasis; emphasizing one dimension caused mode collapse into narrow, low-quality outputs**, and the invalidity-penalty and SFT-init choices did not transfer across model families.

**Ontology learning proper: RL is absent**

- **[measured]** An exhaustive arXiv listing for "ontology learning" + "large language model" (18 papers, 2023-07 → 2026-08, including the axiom-identification benchmark [arXiv:2512.05594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05594) sweep #1 cited, OLLM [arXiv:2410.23584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23584), OntoLearner [arXiv:2607.01977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01977), and the Aug 2026 scale study [arXiv:2608.31118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31118)): 12 prompting/RAG, 6 SFT, **zero RL**. LLMs4OL 2024/2025/2026 ([challenge site](https://sites.google.com/view/llms4ol2026); 2024 overview [arXiv:2409.10146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10146)): no reward-based entries in any edition **[2025 overview PDF unverified]**.
- **[measured]** Evontree (ZJU/Ant, [arXiv:2510.26683](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26683v1)): two ontology rules (subsumption transitivity, synonym→subclass) validate LLM-extracted triples; +3.1% Llama3-8B, +3.7% Med42-v2 across MedQA/MedMCQA/PubMedQA. The update is **self-distillation SFT, not RL** — logical consistency is used as a *filter before SFT*, structurally a gate, not a reward.

**Self-refining ontologies: LLM judge, not reward**

- **[measured]** OaK (Nanjing, 24 Aug 2026; [arXiv:2608.22974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22974)) — sweep #2's X2 item — does **not** use RL: the refiner is a Claude Sonnet 4.6 judge reading kernel + trajectory + task scores and emitting add/delete/modify directives, inference-time only, no human gate. Its numbers are inconsistent across base models: TravelPlanner 59.31% (DeepSeek) vs MemP 63.91% — a loss — but 68.00% vs 54.50% on GPT-4o-mini; CRMArenaPro 78.38%/75.20%.
- **[measured]** PARSE (Amazon, [arXiv:2510.08623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08623)) rewrites JSON schemas by LLM feedback ("the JSON schema is a natural-language understanding contract"); up to 64.7% on SWDE. schema-miner^pro (TIB/L3S, SWJ minor revision; [SWJ page](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/content/schema-miner-pro-agentic-ai-ontology-grounding-over-llm-discovered-scientific-schemas-human)) grounds LLM-discovered schemas to QUDT with **two explicit human expert gates** and no learned reward.

**Self-evolving agent memory: RL is real, the schema is hand-designed**

- **[measured]** Mem-α (UCSD/SFU/Amazon, [arXiv:2509.25911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25911)): GRPO; MemoryAgentBench avg 0.592 vs long-context Qwen3-32B 0.461; reward = QA correctness + tool-format + compression + **an LM-judged "semantic validity" term whose removal is catastrophic**; memory schema (core/episodic/semantic) is fixed. MemRL ([arXiv:2601.03192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03192)) and MemQ ([arXiv:2605.08374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08374)) learn retrieval/write policies over fixed structures.
- **[measured]** MindMemOS (12 Aug 2026; [arXiv:2608.12428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12428)): LOCOMO 94.03%, PersonaMem 70.63%; "validation-driven **evolutionary search** to optimize memory schemas" — the only surveyed system where the schema itself is the search variable — and it still keeps "a human-in-the-loop signal" for correcting misaligned memories.
- **[measured]** The Cognee thread (Markovic, LinkedIn, Aug 2026; Kafkas, [Cognee blog, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.cognee.ai/blog/deep-dives/context-graphs-world-models-and-behavioral-validation)): no numbers, no benchmark, no implementation; an explicit anti-gate stance ("graph first, ontology optional"). **[inference]** As of this sweep, "learnable ontologies → world models" is a market narrative with zero operationalization, from Cognee or anyone else.

**Where RL genuinely learns symbolic vocabulary** — predicate invention in robotics/games: GRAIL (TU Darmstadt, [arXiv:2604.16871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16871)), VisualPredicator (ICLR 2025 spotlight, [arXiv:2410.23156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23156)). **[inference]** The learned symbols are grounded perceptual predicates a human would not sign off on, not domain terms; this is not the same problem as an SME-authored overlay.

**Governance patterns worth stealing (all non-RL)**

- **[measured]** ANNEAL: Governed Symbolic Patch Learning (May/Jun 2026; [arXiv:2605.16309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16309)): four domains, 27 multi-seed runs; ReAct/Reflexion retain 72–100% holdout failure on recurring faults, ANNEAL drives them to 0%; failure-driven typed symbolic patches on a process KG; "governed" = multi-dimensional scoring + symbolic guardrails + canary testing + provenance + deterministic rollback, **explicitly not per-patch human approval**.
- **[measured]** G-SPEC (Dec 2025; [arXiv:2512.20275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20275)): simulated 450-node 5G core, zero safety violations, 94.1% vs 82.4% remediation; ~68% of the safety gain from KG validation, ~24% from **SHACL as a deterministic gate**; 142 ms overhead.
- **[measured]** SciToolAgent-Evo ([arXiv:2607.28692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28692)): a LinUCB bandit gate; acquired tools have their "scientific ontology completed online" with no verification — **[inference]** the concrete picture of what removing the gate looks like.

**Searched but not found** (specific negatives): OWL-reasoner consistency (HermiT/Pellet/ELK) as an RL reward (only backprompting repair loops, e.g. [arXiv:2406.07962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07962); GLaMoR [arXiv:2504.19023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19023) argues RL path-finding is structurally unsuited to consistency); SHACL validation as an RL reward (only G-SPEC's gate); OAEI alignment metrics as a reward for an authoring policy (only classical DQN matching, IJDSA 2023); any system named "OntoRL"; any RL entry in LLMs4OL; any self-play or curriculum over symbolic vocabularies for ontologies (evolutionary search exists only over code; MindMemOS over memory schemas); RL for text-to-OWL/Turtle TBox authoring; RL over ontology versioning/change; any reward-hacking study for schema generation (the BPMN mode collapse is the nearest proxy).

**What it says to Horismos [inference]**: (a) the optimization target "RL-authored ontology" does not exist in the literature — declining it is declining a hypothesis, not a proven technique; (b) where RL touches structure it optimizes whatever the critic measures, and the critic is an LLM judge in every flagship — exactly what a human sign-off is there to check; (c) RL over formal symbolic artifacts is brittle where SFT is not (BPMN collapse; SPARQL SFT > GRPO), so the authoring corpus (doc 13) should feed SFT before anyone proposes GRPO; (d) the strongest results run the arrow the other way — a hand-curated ontology is a superb *reward source* (Princeton 89.33%), which is Horismos's "reward for free" thesis validated externally; (e) the one credible pressure on the gate's *shape* is ANNEAL's staged governance for low-risk additive patches, addressed in §4.3.

### 3.2 RL over knowledge graphs

**Foundations [measured]**: DeepPath (EMNLP 2017; [arXiv:1707.06690](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06690)), MINERVA (ICLR 2018; [arXiv:1711.05851](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05851)) — "the KG *is* the MDP", action set = outgoing edges, sparse binary reward — M-Walk (NeurIPS 2018; [arXiv:1802.04394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04394)), and reward shaping + action dropout (Lin, Socher, Xiong, EMNLP 2018; [arXiv:1808.10568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10568)). **[inference]** Action masking by graph structure has been baked in since 2017; only the policy was ever learned, never the graph.

**Has the LLM era superseded it? [inference]** Yes for question answering over KGs; no for link prediction. The 2025–26 frontier is an LLM policy emitting tool calls against a KG API, trained with GRPO:

- **[measured]** KG-R1 (MIT/IBM; [arXiv:2509.26383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26383)): Qwen2.5-3B, GRPO; WebQSP Hits@1 82.75 (RoG-7B 79.89, ToG-72B 75.67), CWQ 65.31; 3.05× fewer generated tokens than ToG; zero-shot cross-KG transfer 77.50 avg. Reward = turn-level format + **schema-valid retrieval** + answer consistency + global correctness; four schema-agnostic traversal primitives.
- **[measured]** Search-on-Graph-R1 (Montréal/Mila, Jul 2026; [arXiv:2607.18481](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18481)): WebQSP EM 91.7, CWQ 88.7 (best reported), live SPARQL/Virtuoso during training; reward is **multiplicative**: format gate × exact match × turn-efficiency (penalty applied only among correct trajectories).
- **[measured]** KG-Reasoner ([arXiv:2604.12487](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12487v1)) 78.14 Hit@1 CWQ at 30B; KG-Hopper ([arXiv:2603.21440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21440)) 7B beats multi-step 70B but its reward loop depends on external LLM judges; Graph-R1 (ICML 2026; [arXiv:2507.21892](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21892v1)) F1 57.82 vs Search-R1 46.19 with a hard format gate on the answer term; AgentGL (ACL 2026; [arXiv:2604.05846](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05846)) link prediction +28.4 absolute with 3B/7B backbones.
- **[measured]** "Learning to Refine" (Twente, Nov 2025; [arXiv:2511.11770](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11770v1)): a private SPARQL endpoint *is* the environment; RL agent 49.7% vs prompted baseline 32.2% vs one-shot SPARQL 19.7% (47.7% executable); reward −1 malformed, +0.5 correct/−0.2 wrong, −0.1 per execution error, −0.02 per turn.

**Reward shaping from graph structure [measured]**: the Princeton path-alignment result above (ablation: path + negative-binary 82.20% vs binary-only 79.54% at 8B); RewardFlow (HKBU, May 2026; [arXiv:2603.18859](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18859v2)) derives a potential R(s)=γ^d(s) by reverse BFS from successful terminal states over a state graph *reconstructed from rollouts* — ALFWorld 7B 84.6% (+3.9 over GiGPO), 1.5B +12.0, Sokoban 7B +29.6; VPR (Tsinghua, May 2026; [arXiv:2605.10325](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10325v1)) turns symbolic oracles into turn-level rewards (Sudoku 56.25% vs 48.43% outcome-only; Minesweeper 10.39% vs 3.91%; transfer to ALFWorld +3.12, WebShop +3.46) and states its limitation: "extending it to less structured, open-ended environments remains an important challenge."

**KG/ontology as a constraint on the action space**

- **[measured]** "The Mechanism Matters: When Knowledge Graphs Help RL" (Syed, Jul 2026; [arXiv:2607.19616](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19616)) — the controlled ablation this topic lacked. MiniGrid DoorKey-5x5 AUC: no-KG 0.39; KG as state features 0.43; **action masking 0.69** (best case 0.77); reward shaping 0.65; shuffled-KG mask 0.34 (p=0.0001, d=1.08). Dose-response over task-critical relations: masking 0.00→0.27→0.34→0.75. **Under a corrupted KG, shaping stays ~0.36 (degrades gracefully by potential-based invariance) while masking drops to 0.00 at every dose; one missing critical relation blocks 81.6% of opportunities.** A MIMIC-IV sepsis offline-RL case with a UMLS-derived KG at ~60% concept coverage was **null**.
- **[measured]** Graph-constrained Reasoning (Monash, ICML 2025; [arXiv:2410.13080](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13080)): KG-Trie **hard token masking** → WebQSP 92.6 Hit and a **100% faithful-reasoning ratio** (every emitted path exists in the KG) — via SFT, not RL. Graph Explorer (Findings ACL 2026; [ACL 2026.findings-acl.387](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.387/)) enforces "actions may use only IDs shown in the prompt" (Qwen3-8B 74.0/80.2 Hit@1), also SFT. SCAIR (Stuttgart/Bosch; [arXiv:2607.22571](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22571v1)) on a *real* enterprise CMDB (116,369 triples, 19,080 questions): 35.14% vs 25.27% best baseline — far below Freebase numbers.

**KG-based world models and environments**

- **[measured]** WALL-E 2.0 (NeurIPS 2025; [arXiv:2504.15785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15785)): learned action rules / KGs / scene graphs encoding feasibility constraints as the world model; ALFWorld 98% after four iterations; Mars +16.1–51.6% success. No RL. RWML (Microsoft, Feb 2026; [arXiv:2602.05842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05842)) gains +6.9 ALFWorld / +5.7 τ²-Bench purely from a sim-to-real next-state alignment reward; DreamGym ([arXiv:2511.03773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03773)) beats baselines by >30% on WebArena with an LLM "experience model" as the environment. RoomKG (VU Amsterdam; [arXiv:2605.22142](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22142v4)) is the only surveyed environment whose state **is an RDF graph** (DQN per-triple keep/drop; GCN 38.92 vs symbolic heuristic 31.96 vs LSTM 7.60 / Transformer 11.80). The Text World Models survey (Jun 2026; [arXiv:2606.09032](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09032v1)) states it found **no formal quantified comparison** of flat-text vs structured symbolic input on identical tasks.
- **[measured]** Query-based KGQA vs direct LLM (AIRI/Skoltech; [arXiv:2507.11954](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11954v1)): symbolic queries win on compositional/temporal questions (PAT F1 39.0 vs 18.3; LC-QuAD 31.0 vs 21.1) and lose on one-hop lookups (RuBQ 46.8 vs 77.0).

**Searched but not found**: hard ontology-derived (type/domain-range) action masking of an LLM policy *during RL rollouts* (GCR masks but with SFT; KG-R1/SoG-R1 constrain structurally without logit masks); RDFS/OWL/SHACL constraints as an RL mask or reward; "ToG-R1" (does not exist); a typed-graph environment substrate for ALFWorld/ScienceWorld (KGs are agent-side memory there); a three-way controlled comparison flat-text vs typed-KG policy vs pure symbolic; RL-LLM SOTA on FB15k-237/NELL-995.

**What it says to Horismos [inference]**: (a) a typed World as an RL environment is precedented (RoomKG, the SPARQL-endpoint-as-environment paper) and nobody has one with a validation gate and proof trees; (b) Horismos's `rank` contract — "precondition-legal candidates the runtime already computed; it cannot add options" (`bridge/mod.rs`:33-35) — is action masking by construction, and "The Mechanism Matters" says that is the highest-ceiling mechanism *if and only if* the schema is complete and correct over its declared fragment, and catastrophic otherwise: the verified, bounded closure (PRP-005/008) is the precondition, and the MIMIC null at ~60% coverage is the failure mode to name; (c) proof trees are ground-truth derivation paths, a strictly richer object than the entity-coverage heuristic that bought 5-hop generalization; (d) determinism deletes a whole reward term (RWML's sim-to-real gap is zero by construction); (e) calibrate expectations: real enterprise ontologies score ~35%, and symbolic structure loses on shallow lookups — the value case is compositional, audited decisions, not lookups.

### 3.3 RL with verifiable and symbolic rewards

This is the highest-leverage area for Horismos and the most active 2026 sub-field. Four threads: the RLVR canon and its 2026 dispute; verifier quality; process vs outcome rewards and the new cluster of *deterministic symbolic step-checkers as process rewards*; and formal shields / constrained RL, where the symbolic layer constrains exploration rather than scoring it.

**RLVR canon [measured]**: DeepSeek-R1 ([arXiv:2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948)) uses rule-based accuracy + format rewards and states neural reward models were avoided because they invite reward hacking. Tülu 3 ([arXiv:2411.15124](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15124v1)) coined RLVR (binary verifiable reward, +1.7 MATH / +3.3 GSM8K / +1.3 IFEval). The 2026 dispute is whether outcome-only verifiable rewards improve *process* at all: "Does RL really incentivize reasoning beyond the base model?" (NeurIPS 2025; [arXiv:2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13837)) says base models overtake at large k; "RLVR implicitly incentivizes correct reasoning" (MSRA; [arXiv:2506.14245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14245)) rebuts with a step-aware CoT-Pass@K metric; ProRL (NVIDIA; [arXiv:2505.24864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24864)) finds new solution paths after 2–3k steps; "Spurious Rewards" (UW/AI2; [arXiv:2506.10947](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2506.10947)) shows random reward gives +21.4 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (ground truth +29.1) and nothing on Llama3/OLMo2. Absolute Zero (Tsinghua, NeurIPS 2025; [arXiv:2505.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335)) has a single model propose and solve its own tasks with a **code executor as the sole verifier** of both task validity and answers. Rubrics as Rewards (Scale; [arXiv:2507.17746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17746)) is the mainstream answer where no checker exists (LLM judge on rubrics; +31% HealthBench).

**Verifier quality — the precondition, not a caveat [measured]**

- "Before the Model Learns the Bug: Fuzzing RLVR Verifiers" (May 2026; [arXiv:2606.01066](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01066v1)): buggy-verifier false-positive rates math 0.832, **JSON tool-calls 0.869 (schema-only validation)**, code 0.557, vs 0.000 for strict verifiers; black-box search finds an exploit within two queries in 94–98% of trials.
- "LLMs Gaming Verifiers" (TU Darmstadt, ICLR 2026; [arXiv:2604.15149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15149)): RLVR-trained models abandon rule induction and **enumerate instance labels** that satisfy the verifier; the fix is Isomorphic Perturbation Testing (verify under logically isomorphic restatements — shortcuts fail, genuine rules are invariant).
- "When the Reward Suite Is Leaky" (Jul 2026; [arXiv:2607.11022](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11022)): a 15.3% verifier false-positive rate paid ~47.6% of reward for wrong code, yet the held-out gap was only 0.20 points — selection, not learning, of exploits. RL with noisy verifiable rewards (RIKEN; [arXiv:2510.00915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00915)) gives unbiased GRPO corrections given asymmetric error rates ρ₀/ρ₁; RLVεR ([arXiv:2601.04411](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04411), PDF partially readable) reports false negatives more harmful than false positives.
- Automating formal verification with RL (MIT thesis, May 2026; [arXiv:2605.30914](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30914v1)): Dafny's deterministic verifier as reward rose 2.2% → 58.1% — **mostly specification hacking** (trivially permissive postconditions); after filtering underspecified tasks, 9.7% → 31.1%. **[inference]** A sound checker is not a sound reward oracle unless the *constraint set it checks* is tight: gate soundness ≠ ontology tightness. This is why PRP-005's non-vacuity pass matters for RL, not only for authoring.

**PRM vs ORM [measured]**: Lightman et al. ([arXiv:2305.20050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)) — process supervision wins on MATH (78% best-of-N) at the cost of 800k human step labels; Qwen's "Lessons of PRMs" ([arXiv:2501.07301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07301)) — cheap Monte-Carlo step labels are *inferior* and best-of-N evaluation is biased; PRIME ([arXiv:2502.01456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01456)) derives implicit process rewards from outcome labels; the 2026 surveys ([arXiv:2510.08049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08049); [arXiv:2602.09305](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09305v1)) characterize rule-based rewards as "simple and unbiased" but sparse, and name credit-assignment, length, position, and faithfulness bias as the hacking surfaces of learned PRMs. **[inference]** Consensus: verifiable rule-based rewards are the trustworthy spine; the open problem is *density*, which is precisely what a deterministic per-step checker supplies.

**Deterministic symbolic step-checkers as the process reward — the 2026 cluster [measured]**

- **Verifiable PRMs for Structured Reasoning** (IBM Research Europe / DCU, Jan 2026; [arXiv:2601.17223](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17223)): Cochrane RoB2 decision trees as deterministic step verifiers; each step emits an id and a categorical label checked by explicit macros. CochraneForest F1 **76.7 (verifiable PRM) vs 70.2 (verifiable outcome-only) vs 56.1 (neural PRM) vs ≤49.8 (pretrained)**; 89.5% step-final coherence. The authors argue symbolic step verification structurally removes the hacking surface learned PRMs expose.
- **Process-Verified RL for Theorem Proving via Lean** (KAIST; [arXiv:2606.20068](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20068v1)): per-tactic reward φ ∈ {1, −0.05, −0.1} (proof succeeds / fails but tactic locally sound / tactic errors), first-error propagation to downstream tactics, tactic advantage attached to the **first token of each tactic**; MiniF2F +2.5 pp at pass@64; no Lean PRM corpus exists to compare against.
- **VPR** (above): a weakened verifier (MCTS N=100 instead of 10,000) **actively harms** performance.
- **PLVR: Program Learning with Verifiable Rewards** (CoreThink, Aug 2026; [arXiv:2608.28421](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28421)): programs of typed primitives, each layer carrying an "ontology"; contract verdicts localize failure to a primitive; "credit assignment here is a derivation rather than an estimate"; +27.8 points over RL on 30B bases (LiveCodeBench v6, τ²-Bench); loss-guided search 65.6% vs 17.5% uniform over the same type-admissible space.
- FoVer (Penn State; [arXiv:2505.15960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15960)) synthesizes PRM step labels offline with Z3/Isabelle; VeriBound ([arXiv:2606.20740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20740)) gives PAC-Bayes bounds for such PRMs; VeriGate (UMD; [arXiv:2605.30451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30451)) applies process rewards only to verifier-passing steps.

**Formal provers and execution as outcome oracle [measured]**: DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5/V2 ([ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=I4YAIwrsXa); [arXiv:2504.21801](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21801)), Kimina-Prover 80.7% pass@8192 miniF2F ([arXiv:2504.11354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11354)), Seed-Prover 5/6 IMO 2025 and 99.6% MiniF2F ([arXiv:2507.23726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23726)), Leanabell-Prover-V2 feeding verifier output into the reasoning stream ([arXiv:2507.08649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08649)); SWE-RL 41.0% SWE-bench Verified with a rule-based patch-similarity reward ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ULblO61XZ0)); ReViSQL above the 92.96% human mark on BIRD with execution rewards, and the finding that **mislabeled data reverses the learning signal** ([arXiv:2603.20004](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20004v1)).

**Trace-level rewards and the loss-mask convention [measured]**: Search-R1 ([arXiv:2503.09516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)) establishes retrieved-token masking as "critical for stable RL training," with an ablation; the τ²-Bench programmatic state checker is used as a deterministic trajectory reward by Capital One (Aug 2026; [arXiv:2608.10357](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10357v1); 0.25 → 0.44); PayPal's proxy-state evaluation ([arXiv:2602.16246](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16246v3)) substitutes an LLM state tracker + judge (>90% human agreement) because it lacks a deterministic backend — **[inference]** the negative control for a project that has one; self-evolving synthetic tool-use data ships an executable per-instance checker with each task ([arXiv:2601.22607](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22607v1)).

**Perception specifically [measured]**: Perception-R1 (USTC; [arXiv:2506.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07218v3)) — accuracy-only RLVR **fails to significantly improve perception** (McNemar p = 0.22–0.69) while a per-annotation perception reward does (p = 0.04); that reward is LLM-judged, and a weak judge causes "severe reward hacking." Knowledge-to-Verification (Shanghai AI Lab; [arXiv:2605.18261](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18261v1)) uses KG quintuples turned into fill-in-the-blank items with a rule-based validator, answer-gated.

**Formal shields and runtime enforcement — the symbolic layer constraining exploration [measured]**

- Lineage: Alshiekh et al. (AAAI 2018; [arXiv:1708.08611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611)) — preemptive (safe-action list) vs post-posed (veto and correct) shields from LTL safety specs; Reward Machines (JAIR 2022; [arXiv:2010.03950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03950)). 2026: probabilistic shielding with train+test guarantees (AAAI 2025; [arXiv:2503.07671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07671)); robust shielding for uncertain MDPs ([arXiv:2606.00270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00270)); shields for **offline** RL from logs ([arXiv:2605.10293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10293)); `tempestpy` wiring Storm-synthesized shields into Gymnasium ([arXiv:2606.03804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03804), which names formal-methods expertise and "assumes an environment model" as the limiting cost). **The CAV 2026 result** (Radboud/Brno; [arXiv:2605.10888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10888)) **proves a shield cannot simultaneously preserve strong safety and strong permissiveness guarantees** — one side must be made exact.
- For LLM agents: **Agent-C** (UIUC, ICLR 2026 VerifAI-2; [arXiv:2512.23738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23738)) — temporal/state constraints → FOL → SMT interleaved with constrained decoding; non-compliant actions backtrack and resample; **100% conformance, 0% harm, and utility *up*** (Claude Sonnet 4.5 conformance 77.4→100%, utility 71.8→75.2%; GPT-5 83.7→100%, 66.1→70.6%). **The Verifier Tax** (ACM CAIS 2026; [arXiv:2603.19328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19328)) — a blocking-only mediator stops up to 94% of non-compliant actions yet safe success stays **below 5%**; recovery after a block 21% on simple tasks, near zero on complex. **AgentLTL** (Jul 2026; [arXiv:2607.02599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02599)) — one FO-LTL spec serves as offline evaluator, online enforcer, *and* dense training reward (+38 pp accuracy, +17.5 pp compliance from finetuning). AgentSpec (ICSE 2026; [arXiv:2503.18666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18666)): >90% unsafe code prevented, ms overhead, but LLM-generated rules drop to 70.96% recall. The agent-safety survey ([arXiv:2608.14590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14590), also in sweep #2): NL→formal translation 24–35% semantically correct; the specification bottleneck dominates.
- Constrained RL for LLMs is uniformly **soft**: Safe RLHF (Lagrangian; [arXiv:2310.12773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12773)), Certifiable Safe RLHF (exact penalty, training-time only; [arXiv:2510.03520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03520)), Constrained GRPO (Mila; [arXiv:2602.05863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05863)). **[inference]** No hard runtime guarantee exists in the CMDP-for-LLMs line; hard guarantees live only in shielding/runtime enforcement — the line Horismos's gate is on.
- Reward specification in typed vocabulary: "Do It for HER" (Feb 2026; [arXiv:2602.06227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06227)) lifts reward machines to LTLf Modulo Theories over first-order predicates, removing manual predicate encoding; Live LTL progress tracking ([arXiv:2604.17106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17106)) labels subformulas true/false/**open** — **[inference]** a proof-tree-shaped exploration signal. Neuro-symbolic action masking (Utrecht; [arXiv:2602.10598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10598)) *learns* the masks (empirical, not formal, guarantee); Verona's H-PPO variants ([arXiv:2601.02850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02850); [arXiv:2604.25534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25534)) bias rather than block and are tested under imperfect symbolic knowledge.

**Searched but not found**: an RDFS/OWL reasoner, SHACL validator, or Datalog engine as the reward oracle for an LLM policy; training an NL→typed-facts perception model against a downstream deterministic rule-engine gate verdict; an audit trace shipped as an RL corpus with masks precomputed at emission (masks are always reconstructed at training time); a shield synthesized over an OWL/RDFS ontology; a verifier-tax measurement for a symbolic knowledge-base gate; quantified false-positive rates for *formal* verifiers as reward oracles (the reported failure mode is specification weakness instead).

**What it says to Horismos [inference]**: (a) "deterministic symbolic step-checker as process reward" is a validated 2026 pattern, still small enough that a general-purpose kernel doing it for *any* loaded ontology is a contribution — every paper in the cluster hand-builds its verifier for one task; (b) the mechanics are already worked out (three-valued per-step verdict, first-error propagation, first-token advantage) and map onto per-fact gate verdicts and a per-step `loss_mask`; (c) proof trees are the best-theorized, least-used signal (PLVR's "derivation, not estimate"); (d) outcome labels alone will not fix perception; (e) the verifier-quality results make the BDD verifier + non-vacuity a *prerequisite* for using the gate as a reward, and Isomorphic Perturbation Testing (reward invariance under relabeling of the ontology) is directly portable; (f) do not claim "unhackable" — claim "deterministic, auditable, cheap to harden"; (g) the CAV 2026 impossibility says Horismos chose the exact-safety side, so *permissiveness loss* becomes the primary quality metric, and Agent-C vs the Verifier Tax is the quantified argument for mandatory fallbacks (resample a compliant alternative, never merely block).

### 3.4 Trace-to-training flywheels: who does it, what format, what results

**Method note [measured]**: most format details below come from reading framework **source code** via the GitHub API and arXiv HTML, not from snippets; two earlier PDF-summarizer outputs on Agent Lightning v1.0 (OTel span mapping, TD-λ, "~15% improvement") were contradicted by the source and are **not** cited.

**Agentic RL frameworks and their trajectory schemas [measured]**

- **Agent Lightning** (Microsoft; [arXiv:2508.03680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03680); v1.0 [arXiv:2608.17528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17528); [repo](https://github.com/microsoft/agent-lightning)). Unit of training = one **LLM invocation**: `(input_t, output_t, r_t)`; "LightningRL decomposes agent trajectories into transitions … **eliminating the need for additional masking**." v1.0 is an event log — `Event{event_type: "model_request" | "reward", rollout_id, data}` with `ModelRequestData{request, response, usage, finish_reason, model_version}` and `RewardData{value: float, source, reason}` — captured by a proxy: "the harness, rather than the training engine, owns the environment interaction loop"; consecutive calls merge only on **exact token-level prefix match**; reward is outcome-based, "assigned to every sample within the rollout." A code search for `opentelemetry` in the repo returns **0 hits**. Results: SWE-bench Verified **41.8% → 56.4%** (Qwen3.5-9B, 6K examples), HotpotQA 25.1 → 41.7, instruction-following 51.9 → 70.2.
- **Prime Intellect `verifiers` v1** ([repo](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/verifiers); `verifiers/v1/trace.py`): `TRACE_VERSION = 1` — an explicitly versioned wire format. `Trace{version, id, task{type,data,key,hash}, agent{config, runtime, name, trainable: bool}, tools, nodes: list[MessageNode], calls, rewards: dict[str, Reward{score, weight}], metrics, errors, timing}`. `MessageNode` is **per-token**: `parent`, `semantic_parents` (harness-declared DAG edges), `sampled: bool` ("True iff a model call produced this message … False for … assistant/tool messages fabricated as context the model never generated"), `token_ids`, `mask: list[bool]` (trainable, model-sampled tokens only), `is_content: list[bool]` (body vs chat-template scaffold), `logprobs`, `advantages` (`None` distinguishes "never scored" from zero). `Episode` wraps multiple agents with a `PolicySpan.drift` off-policyness measure. INTELLECT-3 (Dec 2025; [arXiv:2512.16144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16144)) was trained end to end on Environments Hub environments with `prime-rl`.
- **OpenPipe ART** ([repo](https://github.com/OpenPipe/ART)): `Trajectory{exchanges (verbatim request/response bodies incl. Anthropic Messages and OpenAI Responses), messages_and_choices, reward: float, metrics, metadata}`; trainable span is structural (`Choice` objects train, `Message` dicts do not; per-token `TokenFlag`); envelope `{format: "art.trajectories", version: 1}`. RULER (LLM-judge ranking of grouped trajectories) and ART•E ("Qwen 2.5 14B beats o3 at email retrieval") are README/blog claims whose bodies could not be fetched **[unverified]**; the W&B Serverless RL "40% lower cost / 28% faster" figures have no published methodology.
- **rLLM** (Agentica/Berkeley; [docs](https://docs.rllm-project.com/core-concepts/episodes-trajectories-steps)): `Step{id, input, output, action, reward, done, prompt_ids, response_ids, logprobs, advantage}`, `Trajectory{steps, reward, signals}`, `Episode{task, termination_reason, is_correct, trajectories}` — reward at both step and trajectory level.
- **SkyRL-Agent** (Nov 2025; [arXiv:2511.16108](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16108v1)): per-invocation transitions (input tokens, output tokens, logprobs); prefix-sharing transitions packed with standard masking; trajectories cut by context/step limits are **masked out of gradient updates entirely**; SA-SWE-32B 39.4% SWE-bench Verified at 4,601 H100 hours vs 9,180 for DeepSWE-32B (36.4%).
- **verl multi-turn** ([docs](https://github.com/volcengine/verl/blob/main/docs/sglang_multiturn/multiturn.rst)): the canonical recipe — delta re-tokenization per assistant turn, `loss_mask += [1] * len(new assistant tokens)`, "only tokens generated by the assistant are included in the loss mask," plus a `tokenization_sanity_check_mode` (default `strict`) because chat-template re-rendering silently shifts token boundaries.
- **TRL GRPOTrainer** ([docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/grpo_trainer)): `rollout_func` returns `prompt_ids, completion_ids, logprobs`; native `tools=` and `environment_factory=`; tool tokens excluded from token *counts*, loss-side treatment undocumented. VerlTool ([arXiv:2509.01055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01055)), GEM ([arXiv:2510.01051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01051), 24 environments), ROLL Flash ([arXiv:2510.11345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11345)): no schema detail in abstracts. AReaL, NeMo-RL, verl-agent: not reached.

**The loss-mask convention is not unanimous [measured]** — a correction to a local premise:

| System | Mechanism | Default |
|---|---|---|
| ToRA ([arXiv:2309.17452](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17452v3)) | implicit: execution output `o_i` appears only in conditioning of the imitation objective (Eq. 4) | mask |

**[inference]** Doc 09's design rule 2 rests on "the TIR/CIR literature is unanimous"; it is not — the corpus whose tag family `EXPORT_TAGS` mirrors is the one that does not mask. The *choice* (mask executor output) still matches the RL-side majority, but the premise should be restated, and the granularity is wrong: every trainer consumes **token-level** masks, and the model-output/scaffold boundary falls *inside* a step once a chat template is applied. `verifiers`' two parallel vectors (`mask` = sampled?, `is_content` = body vs scaffold?) is the sharpest formulation; a step-level boolean cannot express it.

**Trace/log formats near training data [measured]**: OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions ([OTel blog 2026](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/)) carry `gen_ai.input.messages` / `gen_ai.output.messages` / token usage but every `gen_ai.*` attribute is still "Development" stability **[unverified — snippet]**, the post never mentions training, and no training on OTel traces was found anywhere. OpenInference ([spec](https://github.com/Arize-ai/openinference/blob/main/spec/semantic_conventions.md)) is the only observability schema with a native per-span score slot (`evaluation.{name,score,label,explanation,annotator_kind}`) — an eval format, not a training format (no token ids, logprobs, or masks). LangSmith ([docs](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/manage-datasets-in-application)) converts traces to dataset examples (including rules that auto-add traces meeting conditions) and to "industry standard openai formats," positioned for evaluation. **No cross-framework trajectory standard exists**: two arXiv searches for one returned zero results; the de-facto standards are the OpenAI messages list and `prompt_ids / completion_ids / logprobs / mask`.

**Scaffold coupling [measured]**: DCAS (Aug 2026; [arXiv:2608.06113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06113)) — "trajectory datasets used to fine-tune open models are collected almost exclusively under OpenHands," and such models "degrade substantially when deployed under any non-training scaffold" (no numbers in the abstract).

**Deterministic outcome labels as reward, and their failure modes [measured]**: τ-bench ([arXiv:2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045v1)) reward `r = r_action × r_output ∈ {0,1}` (final database matches the unique ground truth × required information present), `pass^k` collapsing to <25% at k=8 in retail; τ²-bench ships a Gymnasium interface with `reward_basis` gating ([repo](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench)). SWE-Gym ([arXiv:2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)), SWE-smith 50k instances / SWE-agent-LM-32B 40.2% ([arXiv:2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798)), SWE-rebench V2 ([arXiv:2602.23866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23866)), Open-SWE-Traces 207,489 trajectories under both OpenHands and SWE-agent → Qwen3-30B-A3B 61.7% SWE-bench Verified ([arXiv:2606.16038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16038)). Failure modes: SWE-TRACE ([arXiv:2604.14820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14820)) — "sparse execution rewards … exacerbate token bloat, reward hacking, and policy degradation"; faulty-code-driven test synthesis ([arXiv:2608.24135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24135)) — "insufficient test case coverage, leading to reward hacking"; PayPal's proxy-state evaluation (§3.3) criticizes τ-bench-style "fully deterministic backends, which are costly to build and iterate." **[inference]** The 2026 complaint is not that deterministic labels are wrong but that they are sparse and expensive to author; a kernel that computes final state and `goal_reached` as a side effect sits on the expensive half of that problem.

**Production "flywheel" claims [measured]**: Cognition SWE-1.5 ([blog](https://cognition.com/blog/swe-1-5)) — "end-to-end reinforcement learning on real task environments using our custom Cascade agent harness," a manually created dataset that "mirror[s] the wide distribution of real-world tasks," speed "up to 950 tok/s"; they "stopped reporting SWE-Bench numbers in 2024" and do **not** claim training on logged production trajectories. **Symbolic execution traces as LLM training data [measured]** — thin, with one cautionary result: "On the Generalization Gap in LLM Planning" (Jan 2026; [arXiv:2601.14456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14456)) — SFT of a 1.7B model on 40,000 PDDL domain-problem-plan tuples from 10 IPC 2023 domains: **82.9% valid in-domain, 0% on two unseen domains**; verifier-reward RL against VAL saturates faster but "does not improve cross-domain generalization"; **symbol anonymization and compact serialization caused significant drops despite preserving plan semantics**. VeriCoT ([arXiv:2511.04662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04662)) uses symbolic checking as a filter over model output. SPEAR ([arXiv:2608.26550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26550)) and RIMRULE ([arXiv:2601.00086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00086)) **[unverified — listing snippets]** use symbolic milestones as dense process rewards and distilled rules injected into the prompt. **No work found on rule firings, Datalog derivations, or proof trees as SFT/RL corpora.**

**Searched but not found**: AReaL, NeMo-RL, verl-agent schemas; any JSON-schema-level trajectory standard; training on OTel traces (positive evidence against); Braintrust/AgentOps/Langfuse flows; OpenPipe blog bodies (JS-rendered); Fireworks/Together/Predibase/Snorkel/Scale/Harvey/Hebbia/Palantir/Sierra/Decagon/Cursor flywheel claims (not reached); Anthropic Agent SDK / OpenAI Agents SDK / DeepSeek Harness trace schemas; WebArena evaluator taxonomy from source; a measured exploit-rate study.

**What it says to Horismos [inference]**: (a) the per-call *transition* is now the dominant training unit, not the masked mega-sequence — each Horismos perception/proposal/narration step is one `(input context, model output, reward)` transition, and action/reasoning steps are context; (b) the cheapest high-value export is Agent Lightning's shape (`Rollout` + `model_request` events + a `reward` event) — trainable in verl today; the richest target is `verifiers` v1's `Trace`, whose `sampled`, `mask`, `is_content`, named weighted `rewards`, and `trainable` map near one-to-one onto Horismos's provenance, `loss_mask`, and `outcome.labels`; (c) the scarce asset is the verifier-grade label at zero marginal cost, not the trace; (d) the XML renderer is a measured risk (DCAS; PDDL re-serialization) — emit a neutral OpenAI-messages rendering alongside the tagged one and treat the difference as an ablation; (e) proof trees are unproven as token targets and better supported as verifier, dense process reward, and narration source.

### 3.6 The closest siblings to Horismos's perception boundary (my own verification)

These are the papers that most resemble "an LLM proposes typed facts; a deterministic checker against a declared schema decides," fetched and read directly.

- **[measured]** Reinforcement Learning for Tool-Calling Agents in FHIR (Knorr, Müller, Bremer, Schweingruber — IDM gGmbH / University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf; May 2026; [arXiv:2605.14126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14126)): a multi-turn CodeAct agent over FHIR as "a directed graph of resources," post-trained with SkyRL (GRPO + DAPO). FHIR-AgentBench: Qwen3-8B 19.7% zero-shot → **76.7% ± 0.9** after RL, vs o4-mini 46.9%, Gemini 3 Flash 51.9%, GLM-5 58.7%. The policy first collapsed to "no-answer" (the easiest reward) before improving. **[inference]** The nearest published thing to "RL over a projection of a FHIR system of record," and its reward oracle is an LLM judge: a deterministic Horismos decision snapshot would be the un-judged substitute.
- **[measured]** Night Shift Nerds at MEDIQA-SYNUR 2026 ([ACL Anthology 2026.clinicalnlp-1.18](https://aclanthology.org/2026.clinicalnlp-1.18/); task: [Codabench](https://www.codabench.org/competitions/12113/), dataset [microsoft/SYNUR](https://huggingface.co/datasets/microsoft/SYNUR)): a **Qwen3-1.7B** model, SFT on augmented CoT then GRPO with "multi-component reward functions that verify output format, concept presence, value type, and value correctness" against a **193-concept clinical schema** under strict exact-match; F1 **0.803 (SFT+GRPO) vs 0.620 (GRPO-only)**. **[inference]** This is the perception boundary trained with a deterministic, ontology-shaped verifier, at sub-2B scale, and the SFT-first ordering mattered.
- **[measured]** Schema-Grounded LLM Extraction for FHIR Patient Digital Twins (Jan/May 2026; [arXiv:2601.05847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05847)): decodes under a JSON Schema "derived directly from FHIR R4 StructureDefinitions" with "a validator-in-the-loop repair stage whose diagnostics are fed back as structured error messages" — inference-time only; evaluated by the readmission-AUROC gap between classifiers trained on generated vs expert bundles (MIMIC-IV, n2c2 2018). **[inference]** Structurally identical to doc 06's schema derivation + witness feedback loop — which Horismos documents but does not implement (§2.2).
- **[measured]** LA-RL (Jul 2026; [arXiv:2607.23420](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23420v1)): GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B with outcome-only rewards r = r_task + 0.5·r_format (+ a first-pass term); SciER relation extraction +6.7 F1, OOD RE +20 F1; no PRM. Minerva (RIT et al., May 2026; [arXiv:2602.00513](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00513v3)): deterministic verifiers over canonical identifiers, label sets, and CVSS vectors — "CTI outputs often have canonical targets … or a structured extraction schema" — 16 tasks, 32k instances, +15.8 over base across four backbones, with heuristic and classifier filters against degenerate traces.

**[inference]** Together with Perception-R1 (§3.3), the lesson for training a Horismos perception model is consistent: closed vocabularies and typed fields make extraction *verifiable*; a deterministic multi-component verifier works at 1.7B–8B scale; SFT on verified pairs precedes GRPO; and the reward should be per-fact (format, class membership, type, constraint), which is exactly the five-check gate's decomposition.

**What it would concretely take** — grouped by the gap it closes (all **[inference]**, each anchored in §2.2's measured state):

1. **Signal completeness in the trace (PRP-004, additive under `trace::migrate`)**: (a) record every gate-refused perception attempt as a `Step::Perception` with `accepted: false` and the `Witness` list (today `trace_step: None`); (b) pass the `Witness` back to the Bridge on retry as doc 06 already specifies (today the identical request is re-sent) — this is both a better inference loop and the negative-example channel a verifier reward needs; (c) add deterministic `outcome.labels`: `workflow_completed`, `gate_refusals`, `retries_used`, `fallback_step`, `proof_depth`, `decision_matches_gold` (when a task supplies one), `goal_unreachable_proven` (the abstention label OpenAI now pays for). Keys may be added within a version per the migrate policy.
2. **Granularity**: emit either per-call transitions (input context, model output, reward) — the dominant 2026 unit — or byte/token offsets for the model-generated span within each step, so consumers get token-level masks without re-rendering; keep the step-level `loss_mask` as the human-readable summary.
3. **An environment + grader surface, outside `horismos-core`** (anti-wrapper boundary intact; the kernel stays network-free and model-free): a Gymnasium/OpenEnv-shaped `reset(ontology, snapshot, input) → observation{schema, text}`; `step(PerceiveResult | RankResult) → StepResult{observation{witnesses, proof, labels}, reward, done}` with rewards exposed as **named, weighted components** (`verifiers` shape) and a scalar for `reward: float` ABIs; plus a `grade(sample, item) -> float` entry point that fits OpenAI's `python` grader envelope (no network, ≤2 min, 2 cores). `MockBridge`-style scripted policies give zero-model CI for the environment itself, exactly as the DoD test does today.
4. **Task supply**: RL needs many tasks with known outcomes. The BDD verifier's witness regions are a *generator* of input cases (a region → a synthetic structured input → a gold decision by symbolic run), and a proven-unreachable goal is a labeled impossible task — Absolute Zero's executor-as-curriculum pattern with a declarative kernel; scenario probes (doc 13) are the same machinery at authoring time.
5. **Exporter as adapter**: the doc-09 `to_training_jsonl` becomes a thin renderer over the environment's transitions with two outputs — Agent Lightning's minimal shape (`Rollout` + `model_request` + `reward` events; trainable in verl today) and `verifiers` v1's `Trace` (near one-to-one with provenance/`loss_mask`/labels) — and two renderings (neutral OpenAI messages; the decided tagged form), treating the difference as a measured ablation (DCAS, PDDL re-serialization).
6. **Replay from snapshot** (doc 09 promises it; unbuilt): per-step snapshot refs make counterfactual scoring of a policy or an ontology delta exact and cheap, which is the asset every world-model paper pays for.
7. **Verifier-quality preconditions (PRP-005)**: measure the gate's false-accept/false-refuse rates on a labeled perception set and publish ρ₀/ρ₁ (the RIKEN correction consumes them); add an isomorphic-relabeling invariance test (rename classes/properties consistently, require identical rewards); make non-vacuity part of every verification pass (already captain-adopted); state the non-gameability property formally: *reward is a total function of post-transaction World state and the loaded ontology, never of output text*.

**Is anyone already doing the equivalent? [inference]** Piecewise yes — each of the four 2026 symbolic-PRM papers, SYNUR, AgentLTL (one spec = enforcer + reward), and the FHIR RL paper (whose reward is an LLM judge) — but every one hand-builds its oracle for a single task. No surveyed system offers a general declarative kernel that yields the oracle for *any* loaded ontology, emits a proof/verdict object rather than a scalar, and ships the audit trace as the corpus with masks precomputed. That combination is unoccupied.

### 4.3 Learnable ontologies vs the gated loop

**Does RL-driven ontology learning change X2's calculus? [inference]** No — it strengthens it. Sweep #2 said the human gate must be defended on safety grounds (stealth-consistent axiom edits; drift), not accuracy. This sweep adds: (i) there is no RL-authored-ontology result to react to (§3.1); (ii) where RL touches structure, the critic is an LLM judge and the objective is utility, not truth; (iii) RL over formal artifacts shows mode collapse and does not beat SFT on the nearest symbolic task; (iv) RLVR-trained models enumerate instances instead of inducing rules when the verifier is extensional — an ontology-verifier reward without an invariance test would be gamed the same way. The evidence supports keeping RL exploration entirely *left of* the admission gate.

**Can RL explore freely in a sandbox while only gate-approved deltas land? Yes — and the kernel's determinism makes the sandbox unusually cheap.** A "learnable ontology, gated commit" architecture in Horismos terms:

```
proposal sources ──► candidate overlay delta (.ttl diff, source-anchored)
  (trace-mining, LLM draft,        │
   RL/evolutionary search —        │  unconstrained; may be an RL policy
   all "neural proposes")          ▼
                         [1] Ontology.load + verify()      ← PRP-005: 4 rule-set checks + workflow
                              witnesses in domain terms       checks + non-vacuity + assumption manifest
                                    │ fail → witnesses back to proposer (the RL reward, dense)
                                    ▼
                         [2] Sandbox: forked World(s) at pinned snapshots, replaying the
                              recorded transaction corpus deterministically (trace equality
                              is a valid assertion) — exact counterfactual evaluation
                                    │
                                    ▼
                         [3] Deterministic score vector (AlphaEvolve pattern: total function):
                              proofs preserved / broken · gate refusals Δ · fallback rate Δ ·
                              previously-refused perceptions now admitted · decision flips (each
                              with its witness) · verifier-tax / permissiveness Δ · replay drift
                                    │
                                    ▼
                         [4] Admission gate (formal, automated): profile conformance, no
                              regression on the proof corpus, determinism preserved,
                              isomorphic-relabeling invariance, novelty vs existing rules
                                    │
                                    ▼
                         [5] Human sign-off on rendered diff + score card + assumption diff
                              (doc 13 review surfaces) → versioned, signed .ttl
```

Properties: the RL loop (proposer ↔ [1]–[3]) is closed and fast; nothing to the right of [4] is ever touched by a learned component; trace-mining stays a *proposal source* exactly as PRP-007 already scopes it; EvoEnv's precondition (verification strictly cheaper and more reliable than proposal) holds for an ontology delta against a deterministic replay corpus. Reward design must follow the BPMN result (equal weighting, no single-metric maximization) and the Darmstadt result (invariance testing). The proposer should be SFT'd on the verified authoring corpus before any GRPO. **Gate-shape question, flagged not decided (§5)**: ANNEAL's staged governance (auto-admit *additive, low-risk* deltas under canary + provenance + deterministic rollback; human sign-off for anything that changes existing semantics) is the one credible pressure on the gate's shape; nothing in v1 needs it.

### 4.4 Usefulness ranking — what would make Horismos most useful to real builders at this intersection

Ranked; effort S/M/L; owning PRP or "owner open" (a captain call, §5). **[inference]** throughout.

1. **Close the trace signal gap — S — PRP-004.** Record refused perception attempts with witnesses; feed the witness back on retry (doc 06 already promises it); add the deterministic labels in §4.2 item 1; emit byte/token offsets or per-call transitions. Additive under the migrate policy; it makes the existing claim true, unblocks every other move, and improves inference quality regardless of RL.
2. **Ship an environment + grader surface — M — owner open.** A Gymnasium/OpenEnv-shaped environment and an OpenAI-style `grade()` over the kernel, outside `horismos-core`, with named reward components, a proof/verdict object in the observation, proven-unreachable as an abstention label, and zero-model CI. First task suite: the existing triage example (this does not touch the PRP-008 first-scenario call). This is the single move that turns "traces are training-corpus-grade" from a claim into something a builder can `pip install` and point a trainer at.
3. **The doc-09 exporter as an adapter — S — owner open (doc 09 says "PRP-003+"; PRP-003 is Done).** Two output shapes (Agent Lightning minimal; `verifiers` Trace) and two renderings (neutral messages; tagged), with the rendering difference measured, not assumed.
4. **Reward-oracle acceptance items — M — PRP-005.** Publish the gate's ρ₀/ρ₁ on a labeled perception set; an isomorphic-relabeling invariance test; non-vacuity in every pass; a formal non-gameability statement; a permissiveness (verifier-tax) number alongside fail-closed cases (extends sweep #2 A9).
5. **The gated ontology-learning sandbox — L — PRP-007, after PRP-005 and PRP-008.** §4.3's architecture; trace-mining and any RL/evolutionary proposer feed it; the human gate stays.

**What NOT to build (and why)**: a differentiable / provenance-semiring "Type 5" extension (no path at LLM scale; the entire 2026 win is on the Type 2–3 side, and it would fork the hard-gate design — sweep #1's lineage page already warned); RL fine-tuning of the authoring agent before an SFT baseline on the verified corpus (BPMN collapse; SPARQL SFT > GRPO); an LLM-judge process reward (the field's fallback where no checker exists — Horismos has one); auto-admission of ontology changes in v1 (SciToolAgent-Evo is what that looks like); a self-reinforcing trace → ontology loop (already a PRP-007 non-goal; confirmed); a bespoke cross-framework trajectory standard (none exists; target the two shapes that do); an RL trainer or model serving (be the grader, not the lab).

## 7. Searched but not found (consolidated)

Per-section negatives are listed at the end of §3.1–§3.5; the ones that matter most for the verdict:

- An RDFS/OWL reasoner, SHACL validator, or Datalog engine used as the reward oracle for an LLM policy — in any of the six sub-sweeps.
- Any RL, reward, or evolutionary loop over the *schema/TBox* layer of an ontology; any "OntoRL"; any RL entry in LLMs4OL; any implementation of the Cognee "learnable ontologies → world models" thread.
- Training an NL → typed-facts perception model against a downstream deterministic rule-engine gate verdict (SYNUR's schema verifier is the closest).
- Hard ontology-derived action masking of an LLM policy *during RL rollouts* (GCR masks with SFT).
- An audit trace shipped as an RL corpus with masks precomputed at emission; a cross-framework trajectory standard; training on OpenTelemetry GenAI traces.
- A shield synthesized over an OWL/RDFS ontology; a verifier-tax measurement for a symbolic knowledge-base gate; a false-block cost reported as a first-class metric (PolicyGuard and the Verifier Tax paper are the exceptions).
- Any public Anthropic environment format, grader interface, or reward function; any OpenAI agentic-RFT or environments product; any frontier-lab-published environment spec.
- Rule firings, Datalog derivations, or proof trees as SFT/RL corpora for LLMs.
- A three-way controlled comparison (LLM over flat text vs LLM policy over a typed KG vs pure symbolic) on identical tasks.
- Not reached before the search budget ended: DeepMind Gemini RL statements and AlphaProof primary; Qwen3/Qwen3-Coder, Kimi K2, GLM, MiniMax, Meta CWM, Nemotron/NeMo Gym, Mistral, Nous Atropos recipes; AReaL/NeMo-RL/verl-agent schemas; Braintrust/AgentOps/Langfuse; vendor flywheel claims beyond Cognition, Databricks, OpenPipe/W&B, Prime Intellect.
